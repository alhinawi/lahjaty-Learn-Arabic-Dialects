# Rural Palestinian pronunciation scope

Reviewed November 22, 2026. Two bilingual feature notes revised in the existing rural Palestinian introduction. No entries added or removed.

## Correction

The introduction previously presented qaf-to-k as a rule for Palestinian villages as a whole. Rural varieties are not one uniform sound system. The revised note limits k to some rural varieties and acknowledges q and g elsewhere. The kaf-to-ch note now also makes its regional and lexical limits explicit. Removed illustrative word spellings rather than treating sound correspondences as proof of exact lexical forms.

## Evidence used

All sources below were fetched and read November 22, 2026.

- Otto Jastrow's Palestinian dialect typology, chapter 13, printed pp265-267, available on the author's Academia page. Distinguishes rural q, k and g reflexes, gives localities, and limits the discussion of kaf affrication to particular areas and environments. Bibliographic publication date not established from the fetched page.
  https://www.academia.edu/6437881/A_Short_Typology_of_Palestinian_Arabic_dialects
- Haifaa Majadly's Baqa al-Gharbiyya field study, Al-Majma 6 (2012), pp37-80, sections 1.1.5-1.1.6. Describes kaf affrication and qaf-to-k in the recorded Baqa speaker, but also retained q in some words. This is local evidence from a small sample, not a population-wide survey.
  https://ojs.qsm.ac.il/index.php/majma/article/download/514/688/2102
- Suhair Sameer Al-Faqeih's Hebron University master's thesis (2010), introduction and limitations, plus section 4.5. Describes g in rural Hebron-area speech and k in several other rural areas. Explicitly warns against generalizing its small regional sample. Mirror copy, not an institutional host; used as corroboration, not the sole basis.
  https://www.manaraa.com/upload/e06a6d38-72ea-4212-8746-a620b73cc326.pdf
- Enam Al-Wer and Bruno Herin's study of qaf in Jordan, Langage et societe 138 (2011), section 2.1 and Palestinian-origin speech in Amman. Supports the narrower central/northern West Bank association of k and the importance of speech-community change. Jordanian findings are not projected onto all villages in Palestine.
  https://repository.essex.ac.uk/11445/1/AlWerL%26S138.pdf

## Other sources checked

- The Tel Aviv University record for the 2022 study of traditional Muthallath varieties reports internal differentiation and an over-70 speaker scope. Only its abstract/metadata were available in this check; no full-chapter claim is made.
  https://cris.tau.ac.il/en/publications/observations-on-traditional-mu%E1%B9%AFalla%E1%B9%AF-arabic-internal-differentiat/
- Peeters' record for Paul Driessen's 2014 statistical study reports multiple qaf realizations. Abstract/metadata only; not used to infer its quantitative results.
  https://poj.peeters-leuven.be/content.php?id=3034654&url=article

## Limits

This is a scope correction, not a completed Palestinian lexical audit. Existing headwords, vowels, transliterations, complete example sentences, the money gloss and the rest of the introduction have not been independently validated by this change. These sources do not establish present-day proportions of speakers. No audio, recordings, numerical expansion or native-speaker verification claim was added. The earlier unresolved Sharqia lexical caveat remains unchanged.
