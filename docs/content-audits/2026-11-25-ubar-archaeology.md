# Ubar archaeological context

25 November 2026

The Dhofar `famous.ubar` note presented a lost frankincense city and its identification with Iram of the Pillars as settled fact. Its example repeated a burial-in-sand story. Replace the note with the supported caravan-oasis description and qualify identification with Ubar as proposed, not established identity with Iram. Withdraw the historical example without inventing a new dialect sentence. This does not pronounce on religious interpretation or prove a universal negative about legends.

## Sources read

- UNESCO World Heritage Centre, Land of Frankincense: https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1010/ . Describes the caravan oasis of Shisr/Wubar and its part in the frankincense trade. Product citation.
- ICOMOS evaluation, September2000: https://whc.unesco.org/document/151528 . Describes Shisr as an oasis/caravan site; records identification as Ubar or Ptolemy's Omanum Emporium as suggestions. Product citation, explicitly dated, not a current visitor guide.
- Blom and colleagues,2006, Southern Arabian Desert Trade Routes, Frankincense, Myrrh, and the Ubar Legend: https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/0-387-44455-6_3 . Open abstract read, full chapter not accessed. Distinguishes the fortress/well site and collapse into a sinkhole from legendary city accounts. Corroborating research only; no full-chapter claims implied.
