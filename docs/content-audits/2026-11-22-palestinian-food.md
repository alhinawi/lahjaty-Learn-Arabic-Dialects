# Palestinian food context review

Reviewed November 22, 2026. Two existing rural Palestinian entries, bilingual context notes only. Headwords, examples, standard forms and entry counts unchanged.

## Maqluba

Removed the unsourced number-one gathering-dish ranking, not its Palestinian context. The replacement describes layered rice and vegetables, often chicken or lamb, cooked in a pot and turned out onto a platter. Reem Kassis describes the preparation and ingredient variation; the Bethlehem Cultural Festival documents a Palestinian cooking session with a chicken-and-aubergine version. BBC's profile of a West Bank home cook corroborates the layers, flip and variation. None establishes a measured nationwide first-place ranking. No medieval origin story, exclusive national ownership or universal Friday habit is asserted.

## Taboon bread

Removed the claim about every village household. The replacement describes the traditional clay-oven/hot-pebble method and its characteristic dimples, not current household ownership or prevalence. The 1989 ethnographic book describes a communal oven and baking dough on hot pebbles; Bethlehem University corroborates clay construction, stones and shared use. Kassis describes the bread's dimples and modern alternatives. The university article cites the same book, so these are not treated as wholly independent witnesses.

Only the last source's abstract and acknowledgements were accessible, not the full article. Its warning about calling all ancient ovens tabuns informed the decision not to add an ancient-oven or oldest-bread claim. Search publication metadata differs from the publisher page, so the latter's displayed date is recorded. No baking safety instructions or advice to collect/heat particular stones is offered.

## Limits

These are preparation/context descriptions, not measured popularity or current prevalence evidence. Historical village descriptions are not present-day access or intactness claims. Neither source recipes nor ethnography validate this site's exact dialect spelling, vowels or full examples. Existing Palestinian and Sharqia lexical caveats remain. No native-speaker-review claim, audio, numerical expansion, spending, outreach or hosting change.
