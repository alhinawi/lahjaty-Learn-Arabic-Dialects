# Dead Sea safety correction

Reviewed November 22, 2026. Existing Palestinian rural and Jordanian entries retained, with two bilingual warning notes replacing unsafe or unsupported context. Removed two buoyancy examples instead of inventing dialect-attested replacements. No entries added or removed.

## Reason

The Palestinian example promised that even a non-swimmer could not drown. The Jordanian example also presented floating without swimming, beside an unsourced treatment claim. Buoyancy must not be framed as protection from drowning. Both notes now explicitly state that drowning remains possible and direct readers to safety/lifeguard instructions, with no head immersion or swallowing water. Removed the loosely stated lowest-point claim and medical-treatment wording rather than expanding this safety patch into a geography or health-benefit review.

## Checked sources

Fetched and read November 22, 2026:

- Tamar Regional Council's specific Dead Sea safety instructions, available in English, Arabic and Hebrew. They prohibit head immersion and drinking the water, and tell anyone who swallows it to seek immediate lifeguard/medic assistance. No publication date established.
  https://www.ma-tamar.org.il/duplicated-1655639771/
- The Times of Israel's October 28, 2021 report of a fatal Dead Sea drowning, including the danger of rolling face down. Used to check the no-drowning claim against an actual reported event, not for medical treatment advice or time limits.
  https://www.timesofisrael.com/woman-drowns-in-dead-sea/
- The DeadSea.com safety FAQ explicitly explains that buoyancy does not eliminate drowning. Tourism-site corroboration only; the local council is the source for the safety instructions. No publication date established.
  https://deadsea.com/faq/

## Limits

This is a plain-language safety warning, not a new dialect phrase or native-speaker attestation. The remaining Palestinian and Jordanian lexicon and examples are not validated by this change. No audio, health-benefit recommendation, entry-count growth or hosting change. The Sharqia lexical evidence gap remains unresolved.
