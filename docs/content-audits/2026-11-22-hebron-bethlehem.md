# Hebron and Bethlehem context review

Reviewed November 22, 2026. Two bilingual context notes in existing rural Palestinian entries. Headwords, standard forms, examples and counts unchanged.

## Finding

The previous broad age comparisons are not disproved by this review: UNESCO's descriptions themselves use very-old-city and oldest-church language. This change adds more concrete historical context rather than calling the earlier comparisons false. It describes the Old Town around the Ibrahimi sanctuary and the fourth/sixth-century phases of the Church of the Nativity. It does not rank cities or churches.

## Hebron

The World Heritage description supports the Old Town's development around the Ibrahimi Mosque/Tomb of the Patriarchs complex and its Mamluk and Ottoman architecture. The note applies that architecture to the Old Town, not every part of modern Hebron, and does not equate the date of the medieval fabric with the beginning of settlement. Glass and ceramics are supported by UNESCO's Creative Cities description and the Hebron Rehabilitation Committee's craft account. The latter distinguishes the histories of glass, pottery and ceramics, so no common origin date was assigned to all these crafts.

No current workshop numbers, opening hours, intactness, freedom of access, preservation promise, product recommendation or tourism advice was added. The designation of the sanctuary follows the existing entry; religious tomb identifications in source descriptions are not converted into archaeological proof.

## Bethlehem

The World Heritage description identifies a first church completed in AD 339 and a sixth-century replacement retaining earlier parts. ICOMOS Palestine gives the same sequence. The new note keeps the two building phases distinct; it does not date all visible fabric to 339 or the whole complex solely to the sixth century. Later alterations and additions are documented in the sources.

A 2012 BBC article gives 399 for the first construction, conflicting with the UNESCO/ICOMOS 339 date. The note uses the heritage sources' consistent date, not an average or an unmentioned compromise. BBC was used as independent reporting on the inscription and as a contradiction check, not as the authority for the first-building date. No present-day visitor count, conservation status, ongoing daily-use promise or route-access claim was reused. Christian traditions about the birthplace remain traditions, not a new factual assertion in the note.
