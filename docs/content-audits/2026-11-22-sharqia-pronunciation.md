# Sharqia pronunciation-note scope

Reviewed November 22, 2026. Two note fields revised in Arabic and English. No headwords, example sentences, pronunciations, audio or entry counts changed.

## Finding

The previous notes described qaf pronounced [g] as a blanket identity of Sharqawi speakers and called coffee the first thing every guest receives. The replacement states only that qaf [g] occurs in some rural Sharqia varieties and varies by locality/speaker. The hospitality generalization is removed.

## Evidence read

Dario Ornaghi, doctoral thesis at Università Ca' Foscari Venezia, cycle 22 (2006/2007 to 2008/2009). Title: Variazione e mutamento linguistico nell'Egitto rurale contemporaneo. Un'indagine sociolinguistica sulla diffusione dell'arabo egiziano standard in due localita' del Governatorato di al-Sharqiyya.

Original PDF downloaded and text extracted November 22, 2026:
https://iris.unive.it/retrieve/1a3cd1b5-8d1c-4021-a957-5949710f58e6/ornaghi_955372_tesi.pdf

Relevant printed pages:

- 15-17: distinguishes rural varieties, transitional areas and areas close to Cairene, including Zagazig. A governorate boundary is not a single accent boundary.
- 17-18: identifies [g] and glottal-stop realizations of qaf as the study's variants; [g] is associated with the studied rural varieties, while urban forms also occur.
- 20: identifies the two field sites as il-Aslugi in Zagazig district and il-Idwa in Hihya district. The study is not a survey of all current residents of Sharqia.

The notes intentionally make a limited occurrence claim, not a present-day frequency estimate. The thesis is historical field research, not proof of current prevalence or any speaker's pronunciation today.

## Unresolved lexical validation

This change does NOT validate the exact existing spellings `جوي` or `الجهوة`, their vowels, or the full example sentences as locally attested. A sound correspondence alone is not evidence for every mechanically transformed word. Those fields are unchanged pending direct lexical evidence or a qualified native-speaker review. No recordings were generated or described as verified.

An exact-form web search found a low-quality dialect quiz lead for `جوي` and no dependable Sharqia-specific lexical source for `الجهوة`; neither was used as evidence. Broader academic overview pages found in search could not be read through their publisher URLs, so search excerpts were not substituted for the original source.

## Limits

This is a correction to explanatory scope, not a completed linguistic audit. Structural tests validate neither the remaining words nor their examples. Other Sharqia entries and broader dialect coverage remain to be reviewed.
